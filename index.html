What Is Gelagen

A gummy built around the idea of cellular renewal

Gelagen is marketed to adults looking for nutritional support for skin hydration and radiance, plus stronger hair and more resilient nails. Instead of positioning itself as a topical fix, the brand frames Gelagen as working from the inside — meant to sit alongside your existing skincare routine, not replace it.

The five ingredients break down into a structural nutrient (Biotin), a pair of cellular-renewal nutrients (Vitamin B12, Folate), an oil-balancing nutrient (Vitamin B6), and a hydration-barrier nutrient (Pantothenic Acid).

One caveat worth stating plainly: while the brand explains each ingredient's general role in some depth, we couldn't locate an independently accessible, per-ingredient milligram breakdown. Inside Every Gummy

All Five Gelagen Ingredients, One By One

Each ingredient is individually named, with its intended role spelled out.

1

Biotin — Structural support

Supplies the raw material behind natural keratin production, reinforcing the core structure of skin, hair and nails — aimed at firmer nails, more resilient hair and smoother skin texture.

2

Vitamin B12 (as methylcobalamin) — Cellular renewal

A more bioavailable form of B12, chosen to support the regeneration of healthy skin cells — aimed at faster recovery from minor blemishes and a more even, brighter complexion.

3

Vitamin B6 (as pyridoxine hydrochloride) — Oil & pore balance

Functions as a metabolic regulator meant to keep oil production in check, particularly around the T-zone — aimed at less shine and tighter-looking pores.

4

Folate — Cellular renewal

Supports the turnover of fresh tissue to replace older cells — aimed at a smoother, softer-looking complexion over time.

5

Pantothenic Acid (as d-calcium pantothenate) — Hydration barrier

Meant to support the lipid production that keeps the skin's moisture barrier intact — aimed at plumper, better-hydrated skin that resists dryness.

One thing to know before ordering: we couldn't find a clearly broken-out, per-ingredient milligram amount anywhere on the pages we reviewed. That makes it harder to line this formula up directly against published research doses — worth factoring into your decision.
